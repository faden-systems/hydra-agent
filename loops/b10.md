# b10 — quiet persistence retries (rebase before push, one notice after five minutes)

Host: VM (hydra-agent). Builder: Hermes launches the coder on the VM, coding account L, Sonnet 5
(`claude-sonnet-5`), up to THREE attempts capped at 50 turns each, queued behind the b8 capture rerun for Hermes's
time. Authority: founder 2026-10-05, Slack C0BUCFJLPS7 / 1791233938.157869 (track b). Spec review before merge; no
build before this spec merges; no automatic merge. A failed third attempt goes to the founder as a diagnosis, never
as a budget extension. After merge the candidate is deployed with `hydra update` (requirement 9).

## Problem and outcome

The supervisor commits the manager's memory (`factory/state.json`, `factory/log/`, `factory/manager-memory/`)
after every turn and pushes it to `main` of the faden clone (b7 requirement 19). Whenever a PR merges while a turn
is running, that push is rejected (`! [rejected] HEAD -> main (fetch first)`), and today the supervisor posts a
top-level `persistence pending (episode <uuid>): push: ...` notice at once, with the raw git stderr including the
`hint:` block, reconciles with a merge commit on a later tick, pushes, and posts `persistence recovered
(episode <uuid>): synced at <sha>`. Six such pairs reached #faden-dev on 2026-10-05; every one recovered within two
minutes, and `main` now carries one `Merge remote-tracking branch 'origin/main'` commit per episode.

Outcome: a failed memory push is retried quietly; every push attempt is preceded by a fetch and a rebase of the
unpushed manager commits onto the remote, so a remote that moved during the turn no longer causes a failure at all
and never a merge commit; a single plain one-line top-level notice goes out only when the memory has still not
reached the remote five minutes after the first failure; the matching recovered line goes out only when such a
notice was posted. A collision that recovers within five minutes posts nothing.

This supersedes, for the reconcile step only, the words "using an ordinary merge preserving both histories" and
"send one top-level notice per failure episode ... successful later reconciliation sends one recovered notice" of
b7 requirement 19. Everything else in requirement 19 stays in force: every Git return code is checked, a failed
step is never reported as synced, no force-push, no reset, no automatic conflict resolution, no dropped files,
unmanaged dirt blocks without touching git, the 60-second retry deadline is respected by every automatic attempt
and never reset by a new turn, `no_repo` stays explicit, local bare repositories in all tests.

## Requirements

1. Fetch and rebase before every push. `Supervisor.persist` runs, after staging and committing and immediately
   before `git push`, `git fetch -q origin` and then, when `origin/<branch>` (the current branch's remote
   counterpart) is not already an ancestor of HEAD, `git rebase -q origin/<branch>` (no `--autostash`, no
   `--force`, no `--onto`). This happens on the first attempt of a turn and on every retry, so the push always
   targets the remote as it is now. A rebase only ever rewrites commits absent from `origin/<branch>`; pushes stay
   plain `git push origin HEAD`; no `--force`, `--force-with-lease`, `reset` or `checkout` of tracked content is
   ever issued by persistence. A failed fetch is a `pending` failure with step `fetch` and no push is attempted. A
   failed rebase for any reason is followed by `git rebase --abort` (its result ignored) and recorded as `blocked`
   with step `rebase`, HEAD and the working tree exactly as before (the unpushed local commit retained, no
   `.git/rebase-merge` or `.git/rebase-apply` left behind, every managed file as the local commit had it), and no
   push is attempted. `Supervisor.sync_repo_before` reconciles a diverged clean clone the same way (fetch, then
   rebase when `origin/<branch>` is not an ancestor of HEAD; a failure aborts and blocks exactly as above); it
   never runs `git merge`. A `persist` that follows a blocked `sync_repo_before` in the same turn or tick returns
   False without a git call and without replacing the recorded step and error (the episode's error stays
   `rebase: ...` or `dirty: ...` as the sync recorded it). After a successful reconcile the remote's commits and the local commits' content are
   both present in the tree and `origin/<branch>` is an ancestor of HEAD. When the clone has no unpushed commits
   the rebase is a fast-forward and the tip of the clone equals `origin/<branch>`.
2. Quiet failure. A failed persistence step (any of `dirty`, `fetch`, `rebase`, `add`, `diff`, `commit`, `push`,
   `exception`) records in `logs/persistence.json`, as today, `status` (`pending` or `blocked`), `error`
   (`<step>: <detail>`, the detail being the trimmed last 500 characters of git's output, kept in full in the file),
   `episode_id`, `retry_after`, `pending_local_sha`, and in addition `since` (the supervisor clock, `clock_epoch()`,
   at the first failure of the episode, kept unchanged by every later failure of the same episode and across a
   restart), `failures` (the count of failed attempts in the episode) and `notice_at` (`null` until requirement 3
   queues the notice). A failure queues nothing in the outbox and posts nothing anywhere; the `log(...)` line stays.
   The first failure after a `synced` (or absent) status opens a new episode with a fresh `episode_id`; a failure
   while `pending` or `blocked` continues the episode.
3. One notice after five minutes. `PERSISTENCE_NOTICE_AFTER_S = 300` is a module constant of
   `manager/supervisor.py`. On every tick (`reconcile_persistence`, which `run_once` calls on every tick including
   a tick with no events) and after every failed attempt, the supervisor queues the episode's notice when all of:
   the status is `pending` or `blocked`, `notice_at` is `null`, and `clock_epoch() - since >= PERSISTENCE_NOTICE_AFTER_S`.
   The check does not depend on the 60-second retry deadline: a tick before the retry deadline evaluates it without
   touching git, and a `blocked` episode (which ticks never retry) gets its notice at the deadline too. The notice
   is queued through `_queue_notice_once(home, dev_channel, text, f"persistence-{episode_id}-notice")` (top-level
   in the dev channel, no `thread_ts`), `notice_at` is set to the clock, and the episode never queues a second one,
   across restarts included (the stable id and `notice_at` both guard it). The text is exactly one line, without a
   newline, of the form `persistence pending for <M> min: <summary>` or `persistence blocked for <M> min: <summary>`
   where `<M>` is `int((clock_epoch() - since) // 60)` and `<summary>` is `notice_error_summary(error)` of the
   episode's current `error`. It contains no `hint:` text and no git hint block, no episode uuid, no full
   stderr.
4. `notice_error_summary(text)` is a module-level function of `manager/supervisor.py` that turns a stored error
   (`<step>: <detail>`) into that one summary line: `<step>: <line>`, where `<line>` is the first line of the detail
   that starts with `error:` or `fatal:` (that prefix removed), else the first non-empty line that does not start
   with `hint:`, else `unknown error`. Whitespace runs collapse to one space, the line is stripped, any URL userinfo
   (`scheme://user:secret@host`, with or without the password) is replaced by `scheme://<redacted>@host`, and the
   result is cut to at most 200 characters after the `<step>: ` prefix. The result never contains a newline or the
   text `hint:`. For the real rejection of 2026-10-05 (`push: //github.com/faden-systems/faden.git\n ! [rejected]
   HEAD -> main (fetch first)\nerror: failed to push some refs to 'https://github.com/faden-systems/faden.git'\nhint:
   Updates were rejected ...`) it returns `push: failed to push some refs to 'https://github.com/faden-systems/faden.git'`.
5. Recovery. A successful push sets `status` to `synced`, `last_successful_push_sha`, and clears `error`,
   `pending_local_sha`, `retry_after`, `since`, `failures` and `notice_at` (all `null`), as today for the fields that
   exist today. When the episode that just ended had `notice_at` set, exactly one line `persistence recovered: synced
   at <sha12> after <M> min` (`<M>` computed from `since` at recovery time) is queued through `_queue_notice_once`
   with id `persistence-{episode_id}-recovered`, top-level in the dev channel; when `notice_at` was `null`, nothing
   is queued. A successful persist that follows a `synced` status queues nothing.
6. Unchanged: the 60-second retry deadline (`PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AFTER_S`) and its rules (a tick before the deadline
   does not touch git; a new turn does not reset it; `blocked` is retried at a turn start by `sync_repo_before`,
   never by a tick); the dirty-tree rule (unmanaged dirt blocks before any git call, managed paths alone never
   block); `no_repo`; `hydra status` still shows `persistence: pending (...)` / `persistence: blocked (...)` from the
   status file; the `log` lines; `persistence_checkpoint` seams; every other supervisor behaviour.
7. Tests the candidate must add or rewrite in `tests/manager` (local bare repositories, a controlled clock, a
   fake poster; never GitHub or Slack): (a) a push rejected by a pre-receive hook that is lifted before five minutes
   recovers on a later tick and the outbox stays empty throughout; (b) a rejection that lasts past five minutes
   yields exactly one notice, on the first tick at or after `since + 300` even when that tick is before the retry
   deadline, the notice being one line with the error's first line and no hint block, and later exactly one
   recovered line, with ticks before, between and after adding nothing; (c) a remote that advanced after the local
   commit is rebased onto before the push: the push succeeds, the remote history is linear (no merge commit), the
   remote's file and the manager's state are both present, and when the remote advanced before the first attempt
   no failure is recorded at all; (d) a rebase conflict aborts, leaves no rebase in progress, retains the local
   commit and its content, blocks, and gets its single notice at the deadline; (e) `since`, `notice_at` and the
   stable ids survive a restart: a new `Supervisor` on the same home never posts a second notice and posts the
   recovered line once; (f) `notice_error_summary` on the real rejection text of requirement 4, on a `fatal:` line,
   on a userinfo URL, and on an empty detail. Two baseline tests contradict this spec and must be replaced by tests
   of the same scenarios under the new contract (names may change): `test_failure_then_recovery_sends_exactly_two_deduplicated_notices`
   and `test_diverged_histories_reconcile_with_an_ordinary_merge` in `tests/manager/test_persistence.py`. Every
   other baseline `tests/manager` statement stays structurally identical (the exit checks this), and the baseline
   suite minus those two tests runs unskipped and green against the candidate code. The exit also runs every
   `loops/b7.acceptance.py` check except `persistence_reconciliation` and `persistence_tick_retry`, which pin the
   merge ancestry and the immediate notice pair; `loops/b10.acceptance.py` re-covers their scenarios (remote
   content present and pushed after a diverged start, the retry publishing the previous local commit with nothing
   new staged, the idle-tick retry across a restart, no duplication across restarts).
8. Notes: `docs/notes/b10.md` with the sections `## What changed` (the persist/sync control flow after the change,
   in prose), `## Evidence` (pytest counts, the acceptance output, the exit's last line), `## Open questions`
   (may say `none`). No Slack ids, tokens or hostnames.
9. Deployment (after merge, Hermes with root on the VM, one controlled restart): create `$HYDRA_HOME/PAUSE`, wait
   until `hydra status` shows no turn running, run `sudo -n hydra update` (which fast-forwards the clone to main,
   copies `manager/` into `$HYDRA_APP` and restarts hydra-manager and hydra-bridge), verify the deployed
   `$HYDRA_APP/supervisor.py` equals `git show <merged SHA>:manager/supervisor.py` by `sha256sum`, remove `PAUSE`,
   and report in the track thread the merged SHA, the digest, the restart time and the first `hydra status` after
   the next manager turn showing no `persistence:` line. The manager records the first PR merge during a turn after
   deployment as the live evidence: no top-level persistence post, no merge commit on `main`.
